SIH26068/Product Requirements/v2 — condensed

WeatherGPT

From weather forecasts to actionable warnings. A conversational weather assistant whose real engine is a warning-first risk layer — not the chatbot on top of it.

Summary
Product
A voice- and text-based weather assistant in English, Hindi and Hinglish that answers “will it rain in my village tomorrow?” with a forecast, any active official warning, a risk level, and a confidence level.
The rule
The LLM never produces a weather number. It only phrases numbers the backend already fetched and scored.
Differentiator
Official CAP warnings from NDMA Sachet are geo-matched to the user and always outrank the conversational answer.
Stack
React + Tailwind → Node/Express + MongoDB → one Python FastAPI service (NLU, risk, uncertainty) → one LLM provider.
Timeline
5 weeks, 6 people. Data and safety layers first; chat UI in week 3.
Cut from v1
Trained ML risk models, GRIB/xarray ingestion, vector search, a worker fleet, an admin console, 10 languages. Reasons in §2.

01What we are building

One sentence a judge should be able to repeat back after your first slide.

WeatherGPT converts forecasts and official government warnings into a location-specific risk level, an honest confidence level, and one recommended action — delivered by voice in Hindi or English.

Everything below serves that sentence. Anything that does not is out of scope.

The problem statement asks for a conversational weather assistant. Roughly forty other teams will build exactly that: a chat box, a weather API, and a translate call. It works on stage and it is reproducible in a weekend, which is why it does not win.

The defensible version keeps the same surface and puts three layers underneath it:

  • Warning intelligence — parse official CAP alerts, match them to the user's district, surface them above the routine answer.
  • Risk translation — turn 96 mm / 62 km/h into “flooding likely in low-lying areas; delay travel.”
  • Uncertainty — say when the models disagree instead of pretending a 7-day forecast is a fact.

Those three are the pitch. The chatbot is the delivery mechanism.

02Scope, and what we deliberately cut

The v1 document specified about eleven months of work. This is the version that ships in five weeks and still demos every claim.

In scope — MVP
  • Natural-language weather Q&A, text and voice
  • English, Hindi and Hinglish (romanised Hindi), end to end
  • Location resolution: GPS, city, district, village name
  • Hourly + 7-day forecast from a live source
  • CAP warning ingestion, geo-match, and priority display
  • Rule-based risk engine on published IMD thresholds
  • Model-disagreement confidence score
  • Source + issue-time provenance on every answer
  • Saved locations and push alerts for severe events
  • Mobile-first PWA
Phase 2 — after the prototype round
  • Marathi, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu and the rest (config entries, not rewrites)
  • Historical / climate-trend module via the archive API
  • Warning polygons and rainfall layers on the map
  • Persona-tuned advice (farmer, traveller, outdoor worker)
  • District dashboard for administration users
  • SMS delivery for feature phones

Why Hinglish is one of the three, and not an afterthought

Most people in small-town and rural India do not type in Devanagari. They type “kal barish hogi kya” on a QWERTY keyboard — Hindi words in Latin script. A product that accepts only क्या कल बारिश होगी and will it rain tomorrow silently excludes the largest group of the people it was built for.

Supporting it is cheap and the payoff is disproportionate: a romanised-Hindi keyword list distinguishes Hinglish from English at parse time, answers are composed in Hindi and transliterated back to Latin for display, and the hi-IN speech voice reads the Devanagari form so pronunciation stays correct. Three modes, one pipeline. Say this on stage — it is a real accessibility insight, not a feature list item.

Cut, and why — say this out loud if a judge asks

  • Trained ML risk modelThere is no labelled “this weather caused this harm” dataset for Indian districts. A model trained on nothing is indefensible in five minutes of questioning. IMD's published thresholds are auditable, correct, and explain themselves. ML goes in Phase 3, on data we will have collected by then.
  • GRIB / NetCDF + xarray ingestionRaw numerical model grids are hundreds of gigabytes and weeks of pipeline work. Open-Meteo already serves the same model output as JSON for a lat/lon. We buy the processed layer instead of rebuilding it.
  • Vector search, RAG, FAISS, embeddingsWeather is structured data, not a document corpus. There is nothing to retrieve semantically. Adding a vector DB adds latency and a failure mode for zero accuracy gain.
  • Redis + BullMQ + four worker servicesOur workload is two scheduled jobs: refresh warnings, send alerts. node-cron inside the API does that. Redis stays only as a response cache, and even that is optional for the demo.
  • Monorepo with packages/ and shared typesThree folders. Tooling setup that pays off at 30 engineers costs us a week and returns nothing at six.
  • Multi-provider LLM abstractionOne provider behind one file with one function. Swapping providers means editing that file. That is the abstraction.
  • Ten Indian languagesThree modes done well beats ten done by machine translation with broken weather terminology. English, Hindi and Hinglish cover the demo; every other language is a config entry we can add live on stage to prove the architecture scales.
  • Admin dashboard, RBAC, feature flagsOne /health page showing source status and last ingestion time. Judges never log in as admin.
  • Native mobile appAn installable PWA gives us push notifications, offline caching, and a home-screen icon without a second codebase.

Non-goals

WeatherGPT does not issue warnings, replace IMD or NDMA, or make disaster-management decisions. It is a decision-support and accessibility layer over official information, and the UI says so.

05What happens when someone asks a question

One request, in order. Note that warnings are fetched in parallel with the forecast, not after it.

  1. Capture — text, or mic → Web Speech (hi-IN) → transcript.
  2. Detect language — script heuristic first (Devanagari → Hindi); LLM only on ambiguity.
  3. Parse — POST /nlu/parse returns intent, location string, time window, and requested variables as strict JSON.
  4. Resolve location — match against our seeded India gazetteer (state / district / town), fall back to a geocoder, fall back to GPS. Returns lat, lon, district, state.
  5. Fetch, in parallel — forecast for the point across three models; active warnings intersecting the district.
  6. Score risk — POST /risk/score. Warning severity floors the result (§8).
  7. Score confidence — POST /uncertainty/score from model spread and lead time (§9).
  8. Compose — build the grounded context object; LLM returns structured JSON, never free text (§10).
  9. Render — warning card first if one is active, then answer, forecast card, risk card, sources.
  10. Speak — TTS reads the summary plus, if present, the warningMessage.

Target: under 2 s for the data path, under 5 s including the LLM. Steps 5–7 are pure I/O and arithmetic; only step 8 is slow, so stream it.

Source hierarchy

Official warnings outrank observations, which outrank model forecasts, which outrank derived estimates. A lower tier may never contradict a higher one in the rendered answer.

07Warning engine

The single highest-value component. Build it in week 1.

A cron job polls the CAP feed, parses each alert, extracts geometry and validity, deduplicates on identifier, and writes it. A second job expires alerts past validUntil. Nothing about this is AI, and that is the point.

Two hard rules

  • Official text is immutable. The LLM may add a translation and a plain-language gloss beside the official text. It may never edit, summarise away, soften, or re-time it. The UI labels the two blocks separately: Official Warning and What this means.
  • A warning interrupts. If a matching active warning exists, it renders above the answer even when the user asked something unrelated. An expired warning never renders as active — check expires at read time, not just at ingest.

Geo-match is one query: { "area.geometry": { $geoIntersects: { $geometry: userPoint } }, status: "active", expires: { $gt: now } }. Where a CAP alert carries only a district name and no polygon, match on our gazetteer district ID instead.

Deterministic, published thresholds. Every number here is defensible because IMD publishes it.

Heatwave thresholds shift for coastal (≥ 37 °C) and hill (≥ 30 °C) stations — one field on the location record selects the band.

Derived risks

Three composites, each a small pure function:

  • Flood — forecast 24 h rainfall, plus observed 72 h antecedent rainfall, plus an urbanFloodProne flag on the location. Saturated ground turns moderate rain into a high risk; this is the rule that makes the engine look intelligent.
  • Travel — rainfall intensity, wind gust, visibility.
  • Outdoor / farm — rainfall timing within the day, heat index, wind.

The safety floor

An active official warning sets a minimum risk level that computed values can raise but never lower.

Yellow → at least MODERATE. Orange → at least HIGH. Red → EXTREME. If our model says calm and IMD says orange, the user sees HIGH. Write this as one line of code and one unit test, and point at it during the demo.

The response carries a 0–100 score with its components exposed (rainfall 35 + warning 25 + wind 15 + antecedent 8 + duration 7 = 90) so the risk card can show its own arithmetic. Present the weights as our engineering configuration, not as meteorological law.

09Uncertainty

Cheap to compute, and almost nobody else will do it.

Request the same point from three models in one Open-Meteo call — models=ecmwf_ifs04,gfs_seamless,icon_seamless. Compare their 24-hour precipitation totals. Confidence falls out of two numbers: the spread between models, and how far ahead we are forecasting.

ConfidenceModel spread (CoV)Lead timePhrasing
HIGH< 20%< 24 h“Rain is likely tomorrow evening.”
MEDIUM20 – 50%24 – 72 h“Rain is expected, but models disagree on how much.”
LOW> 50%> 72 h“Too early to say confidently — check again tomorrow.”

The UI shows the reasons, not just the label: three models agree on timing · they differ on intensity by 40 mm · forecast issued 09:00, 31 hours out. That paragraph is the whole “explainable AI” claim, earned honestly.

10Grounded answer contract

The LLM receives facts and returns fields. It is a translator, not an oracle.

Anti-hallucination rules, enforced in the prompt and in code

  • Every number in the output must appear in the input. Validate before rendering; on a mismatch, drop to the structured card and skip the prose.
  • Missing data produces “I don't have reliable forecast data for this location right now” — never an estimate.
  • The model may not name a source, an issue time, or a warning that was not supplied.
  • User text can never redefine system rules. Treat the question as data.
  • If the LLM call fails entirely, the UI still renders forecast, warning, risk, and confidence cards from the same context object. The product degrades to a very good weather app, not to nothing.

14Definition of done, and the demo

Done means all of this is true

  • A Hindi voice question returns a spoken Hindi answer with correct location and time.
  • An active CAP warning renders above the answer, with its official text unedited and its source linked.
  • An expired warning never renders as active. There is a test for this.
  • Risk level, score breakdown, and confidence with reasons appear on screen.
  • The safety floor is visibly enforced when a warning outranks the computed risk.
  • Killing the LLM key still produces a working answer from the structured cards.
  • Every answer shows its source and issue time.
  • The whole flow works on a 320 px phone screen.
  • The evaluation set runs and reports intent, location, time, and grounding accuracy.

The 90-second demo

  1. Speak in Hindi — “क्या कल मेरे गाँव में बारिश होगी?” Nobody types.
  2. Village resolves — not a city. Say the word “village” out loud; that is the accessibility claim.
  3. Warning interrupts — the orange card appears above the answer with official text and source.
  4. Risk card — HIGH, with the score arithmetic visible.
  5. Confidence — MEDIUM, with “models disagree on intensity.” This is the line judges remember.
  6. Action — one Hindi sentence: cover the harvested crop, delay irrigation.
  7. Spoken answer — audio plays.
  8. Change location live — judge names any district; it adapts. This proves it is not hard-coded, which is what they are actually testing.

Rehearse a no-network fallback with cached data. Assume the venue wifi fails, because it will.

15What judges will ask

Six answers to have ready. Everyone on the team should give the same ones.

How is this different from the IMD app plus ChatGPT?

The IMD app shows warnings but cannot answer a spoken question in Hindi about a village. ChatGPT can converse but has no live forecast, no official warnings, and will happily invent a rainfall figure. We are the layer that joins them — and refuses to state a number it did not fetch.

What stops the AI from hallucinating weather?

Architecture, not prompting. The model never queries a weather source; it receives a JSON object and phrases it. We validate that every number in the output appeared in the input, and we render structured cards even when the model is unavailable.

Where do the risk thresholds come from?

IMD's published rainfall categories and heatwave criteria — 64.5 mm for heavy, 115.6 for very heavy, 204.5 for extremely heavy. We chose the weights that combine them; we did not invent the science, and the card shows its own arithmetic.

What if your risk model disagrees with the official warning?

It cannot lower it. An active orange alert sets a floor of HIGH regardless of what we compute. One line of code, one test, and it is the first thing we show in the safety section.

Are you using real IMD data?

Official warnings come from NDMA's Sachet CAP feed, which carries IMD, CWC and state authority alerts. Forecasts come from Open-Meteo's model output, because IMD publishes no reliable public forecast API — and we would rather say that plainly than claim an integration we do not have.

Can this scale to all of India?

It already does. Nothing is region-specific: the gazetteer covers every district, the CAP feed is national, and the forecast source takes any lat/lon. We ship English, Hindi and Hinglish; adding Marathi or Tamil is a config entry, a glossary and a TTS voice, not a rewrite.
